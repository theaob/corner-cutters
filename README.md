# Corner Cutters

An arcade F1 race in the HD-2D look: start lights, laps, positions, a minimap and results, against up to nine AI cars. Mobile-first portrait layout with a touch control deck.

**Circuits:**
- **Crescent Park**: anticlockwise, flat out almost everywhere, a lap of about 25 s.
- **Silver Heath**: clockwise and fast, with a hairpin and a tight complex as the places to lift; a lap of about 31 s. Its outline is traced from circuit outline data by [bacinger/f1-circuits](https://github.com/bacinger/f1-circuits) (MIT).
Grandstands line each main straight behind the start line, and on the circuits in the country (Crescent Park, Silver Heath, Royal Park, Ardennes) they stand round the outside of the bends too, behind the gravel and the tyre walls, facing the apex: each with its own roof colour and flags waving on top, never so tall they hide the track from the camera (`src/f1/stands.ts`).
- **Harbour**: a street circuit after the most famous one of all, clockwise through a harbour town, its corners in order (eased apart so the track fits between the walls; the outline after the same data): the main straight beside the harbour with the pits on the harbour side, a right at the first corner, the climb up the hill, a long left and a right across the square at the top, down to a tight right and the slowest hairpin of all, two rights to the sea front, the long right-curving tunnel, the chicane at its exit, a fast left along the quay, the pool's left–right and right–left, the tight right at the bottom and the last right onto the straight; a lap of about 28 s. Steel barriers a step off the kerbs all the way round (pavement between, no gravel), a rich old town packed right up to the barriers (stone and ochre houses with arched, shuttered windows, here and there a balcony, under terracotta or flat roofs, some with battlements, and stone towers with tiled spires or battlements; tall, but never so tall that one hides the track from the camera), the casino in the loop above the hairpin (copper-green domes, a pillared porch, a garden with a fountain and palms), an open-air swimming pool beside the pool section with swimmers doing lengths, a clay tennis court below the climb with a rally on, the sea with yachts along the quay, and the tunnel's see-through roof with its lamps. Easy on tyres: in 5 laps a stop pays for some and not others, so track position counts.
- **Royal Park**: after the temple of speed, clockwise through an old royal park, its corners in order (eased for the arcade handling): the longest straight of all (a quarter of the lap), the first chicane (a hard right, then left: the hardest stop), the long right of the big curve, the second chicane (left, right), the two rights of the woods, the straight under the trees, the fast left–right–left chicane, the back straight and **the banking**: a half circle of a right onto the main straight, the track tilting up toward the outside (a grade of 0.3, flat up to its inside edge, easing in and out over 240 px), on concrete run-off with seams running round it, up to a concrete wall and catch fence; a car on it is pushed toward the inside, as on any banked bend (`banking` in the layout; heights in `src/f1/circuit.ts`). **The floating podium** (`podiumDeck` in the layout): a deck hanging out over the main straight from a tower behind the garages, just past the line, with the podium's steps on it, red fascia and glass rail; at the ceremony the top three spray champagne up there while the tifosi flood the track below, jumping and waving flags. A lap of about 30 s.
- **Ardennes**: the longest lap of all (about 13,600 px, 42 s), clockwise, up and down through a forest in the Ardennes, traced from the most famous circuit in those hills (outline data from the same source, its three tightest bends opened out so the track's edges never cross, and a few medium bends eased so a player on the touch stick, with no brake pedal, can live with the AI): the tight right of the hairpin at the top, the plunge past the pits to the bottom of the valley, then the steepest climb anywhere (a grade of 0.18) through the left–right–left at its foot, on up the long straight to the top of the hill, the right–left chicane and the right after it, the long right of the second hairpin, down through the double-apex left, the right–left in the woods and the two rights at the far end of the valley, the long, flat-out climb back through the left kinks, and the right–left of the last chicane onto the straight; 116 px from the top of the hill to the bottom of the valley. A set of tyres lasts three laps here (as on Silver Heath, wear runs by the second, so the long laps wear tyres less per second), so a 5-lap race needs one stop at most. **The forest** (`forest` in the layout, `src/f1/forest3d.ts`): thousands of spruces and firs, with here and there a broadleaf turning gold and rust, packed all round beyond the barriers on a dark forest floor and on out past the edge of the map; each only as tall as it can be without hiding the track from the camera, counting the hillside (a tree on the slope above a stretch of track has less room than one below it), and none on the pits, the garages or a grandstand. Drawn in instanced chunks, so those off screen aren't drawn.

**Teams:** eleven, each in its own colours: Milk Energy, Prancing Monkey, Golden Arrows, Calrissian Racing, British Lime, Reneé, Frankie's Groove, Cheaper Milk, DMW – DEUTCHE MOTOR WERKE, MaaS and Grandma's Fave (`src/f1/teams.ts`), each with its own logo (`src/f1/logos.ts`), shown on the menu's TEAM row and under the start lights. So teams tell apart from afar, every car carries its team's pattern along the top (a stripe, twin stripes, bands, chevrons, halves, a split or a coloured nose; teams sharing a pattern have far-apart colours), and teammates by the T-camera on the air intake: dark on the team's first car, bright green on its second. Each team has two drivers, by their three-letter codes: Milk Energy VER and HAD, Prancing Monkey HAM and LEC, Golden Arrows RUS and ANT, Calrissian Racing NOR and PIA, British Lime ALO and STR, Reneé GAS and COL, Frankie's Groove ALB and SAI, Cheaper Milk LAW and LIN, DMW HUL and BOR, MaaS OCO and BEA, Grandma's Fave BOT and PER. Pick yours on the menu's TEAM row; a race runs five teams of two: yours (you in the first seat, the team's second driver beside you) and four drawn at random. The second driver's car has the green T-camera. Your driver wears a shiny gold helmet (every other driver's is white), and on the grid your car is also marked by a gold ring on the ground round it and a gold arrow bobbing above it, until the lights go out. The results show each car's team.

**Damage and the safety car:** every car, yours and the AI's, takes damage from walls, landings and contact. A damaged car smokes, then burns, and loses up to 30% of its top speed; your health shows as five blocks in the readout. A wrecked car is cleared off the track and classified DNF. A big crash (a wreck, or one hit taking 40% of a car's health) brings out the safety car: it joins ahead of the leader, the field queues behind it on a 200 px/s limiter with no overtaking (and nobody gets past the safety car itself), and after 12 s with the leader lined up behind it, it goes in. Passing a car under it costs 5 s a place, added at the flag. The rules are in `src/f1/raceControl.ts`.

**AI racecraft:** the AI races the cars round it (`aiInput` in `src/f1/racing.ts`, tuned in `RACECRAFT`). Quicker than a car ahead on a straight, it pulls out and passes on the side with room, but only a pass it can finish before the braking zone (its speed advantage, the momentum it carries and the tow, against the straight left); it holds its line till it's a length and a half clear, and tucks back in if the straight runs out first. Caught on a straight by a quicker car still in line behind, it makes one move to cover the inside of the next bend (never across a car alongside). A car alongside isn't turned in on, and side by side into a bend the one a nose behind gives way. Off its usual line in a bend it takes the bend at the speed its radius there allows. None of it while the pack is still bunched from the start (the first 15 s) or under the safety car, except giving way side by side into a bend, which holds at the first corner too (not under the safety car). Each driver's racecraft comes from the difficulty (EASY 0.3, NORMAL 0.6, HARD 0.9, each driver a little either side): the higher, the bolder its passes and the harder its defending (on EASY it doesn't defend). The grid is mixed a little from the race's seed, as after a qualifying with its mistakes, so some quicker cars start behind slower ones and race their way forward.

**Driver personalities and mistakes:** each named driver races in a style (`src/f1/drivers.ts`): a CHARGER (bold, a little error-prone), a METRONOME (measured, rarely wrong), a VETERAN (canny and consistent), a DEFENDER (hard to pass) or a ROOKIE (keen, and the most mistakes). A style adds to the racecraft the difficulty gives, and sets how often the driver gets a bend wrong. Now and then, going into a braking bend, an AI driver makes a mistake: brakes too late (in too fast, then runs wide and gathers it up past the apex) or runs wide through it, each costing a few tenths and a chance for the car behind; more on EASY (8% a bend before style) than on HARD (2.5%), none while the pack settles or under the safety car. The dice come from the race's seed, so a race plays again the same. A mistake near you is called out: LOCK-UP · LEC, or HAD RUNS WIDE. The styles are one table, easy to change.

**Difficulty:** EASY, NORMAL or HARD, in the menu's SETTINGS and remembered (`src/f1/difficulty.ts`). It sets how much a crash costs every car (the damage from walls, landings and contact, and how much a damaged car slows) and how quick and closely matched the AI field is: EASY halves crash damage against a slower, spread-out field; HARD takes 1.75× the damage against a field at 99% of the racing line's pace, closely matched.

**Tyres:** every car's tyres wear as it drives: with speed, faster while sliding (a drift eats them) and on grass or gravel (`src/f1/tyres.ts`). Worn tyres grip and turn less and lose top speed, a little at first, then sharply past about 70% wear; gone, they cost about a fifth of a lap. A set lasts about two laps at its best, so races are 5 laps by default: in 3 laps no stop pays, in 5 one does, in 8 two. The readout shows your tyres: the compound, blocks and the share left (WORN past the cliff), in the compound's colour.

**Weather:** DRY, DAMP or WET, on the menu's WEATHER row and remembered (`src/f1/weather.ts`). Three tyre compounds, each marked by a coloured band round every car's tyres, as in F1: **yellow slicks** for the dry, **green intermediates** for a damp track, **blue full wets** for rain. The wrong tyre grips less and is slower (wets on a dry track also wear out fast), and a wet track is slower than a dry one even on the right tyres: in AI races, damp is about 5% slower and wet about 11%. The crews fit the right compound for the weather at the start and at every stop. Damp and wet races are overcast with a darker track, spray behind the cars and every car's red rain light blinking at the back; wet has falling rain. Lap records are kept apart for each weather.

**Slipstream:** close behind another car at speed, you're in its wake: up to 6% more top speed, strongest right behind it and fading with distance (gone at about five car lengths) and off to either side. The tow builds over half a second and fades soon after you pull out, so pulling out to pass carries the extra speed alongside: the slingshot. TOW in the readout fills as it builds, with a rush of air. The AI is towed too, but uses the extra speed only while there's room ahead to brake from it. None under the safety car, in the pit lane or after the flag. The rules are in `src/f1/slipstream.ts`.

**Controls lap:** a new player's first launch opens a lap of Crescent Park on their own before the menu, with a prompt at a time for the controls on the device they're using (the touch stick, keys or a gamepad): go, full speed, slowing for a bend, drifting (it moves on after two bends without one), keeping inside the white lines, and on round to the line. Each prompt moves on once you've done it; a cut is explained (in a race: a warning, then +5 s). A skips it at any time, and once it's done A goes to the menu; either way it isn't shown again on its own. CONTROLS LAP on the settings screen plays it again. Anyone who has already played (records, a season, a circuit unlocked) goes straight to the menu. The rules are in `src/f1/onboarding.ts`.

**Championship:** MODE: CHAMPIONSHIP on the menu leads to the Championship screen. A season is a round on each circuit in turn, with your team, difficulty, weather and qualifying from the menu, against the same field all season: your team and four others, each driver with the same pace every round. Points as in F1 for the top ten (25, 18, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, 1; none for a DNF), and the drivers' standings by points, then by the best results. Between rounds the screen shows the rounds raced (your place in each), the standings (points, wins, last result) and RACE ROUND n; after the last round it crowns the champion. A round counts once you've seen its results (A or START); leaving it before then doesn't count it, and a finished round can't be restarted. **Circuit unlocks:** only the first circuit is open for a Quick Race or a Time Trial at first; each later circuit unlocks once a Championship reaches it (you've raced the round before it), said on the Championship screen. A locked circuit is dimmed on the menu (LOCKED · REACH IT IN A CHAMPIONSHIP). Anyone who already had a record on a circuit keeps it open, and `?circuit=` opens any circuit (for testing). The season is kept on the device after every round, so it can be picked up later; NEW SEASON (pressed twice while one is running) starts again. The rules are in `src/f1/championship.ts`.

**Time Trial:** MODE on the menu picks a QUICK RACE or a TIME TRIAL (remembered). A Time Trial is flying laps on your own, from the same standing run-up as qualifying, on tyres that don't wear. Each lap is timed from the line; a cut across a marked corner deletes it. At each sector the banner gives your split against the lap you're chasing: purple when it's quicker than your record, green when it's quicker than your best this session, amber when it's slower. Your record lap on each circuit (and in each weather) is kept on the device as a ghost: a see-through car that drives it again beside you from the line, with the live gap to it (GAP, green ahead, red behind) in the readout. START goes back to the run-up; SELECT back to the menu. The rules are in `src/f1/timeTrial.ts`.

**Qualifying:** turn QUALIFYING ON on the menu (it's off by default, and remembered) and the race weekend starts with one flying lap on your own. You start standing on the straight before the line, with about 5 s of driving to get going and get your bearings (TIMING STARTS AT THE LINE); the lap is timed from the line (FLYING LAP) back round to it. A cut across a marked corner deletes the lap (LAP DELETED) and the next one is timed afresh. Your time goes up against the AI's (each worked out from a reference lap on the racing line and the driver's pace, a little either way), and the table sets the grid in time order: pole to last, no time at the back. A or START goes to the grid; A during the lap skips qualifying and you start mid-grid, as with it off. Restarting a race (START, or RESTART on the pause screen, or START on the results) runs it again from the same grid, without qualifying again; to qualify afresh, restart during qualifying or pick the circuit again. The rules are in `src/f1/qualifying.ts`.

**Balance:** `tests/balance.test.ts` measures the difficulties headless with a reference player (`src/f1/referencePlayer.ts`) driving through a player's own inputs (the touch stick, which has no brake pedal, or the wheel). On a flying lap the quickest AI car matches a very good lap on HARD and a good one (95% of the racing line's speed) on NORMAL; on EASY it's slower than a steady player (90%). In a 5-lap race from mid-grid a good player wins on EASY by seconds, a steady one misses the podium on HARD, and a very good one races at the front on HARD (over four races, on average in the top four). Change the AI or the cars and these tests say whether the difficulties still hold.

**Track limits:** the kerbed bends are marked corners, each with a yellow-and-black strip just off the inside at its apex. Cut across one with all four wheels past the inside edge (on the kerb, or two wheels over, is fine; so is running wide on the outside) and it's a strike, one per time through the corner: the first two are warnings (TRACK LIMITS · WARNING 1/2, in amber, with a buzz), and every cut after adds 5 s to your finish time, in red. LIMITS in the readout keeps count. The rule applies to every car, but the AI keeps to the track. The rules are in `src/f1/trackLimits.ts`.

**Pit stops:** each circuit has a pit lane beside its main straight, wider than the track (a fast lane, and a box for each team beside it, the garages behind), behind a pit wall. Long entry and exit roads branch off the track and rejoin it, smoothly, 360 px each way, with the boxes between the two speed-limit lines; the whole lane lies on the main straight. **The shortest pit lane** is the two roads and the boxes' run, 1,040 px (`PIT_LANE_MIN` in `src/f1/pits.ts`), and every circuit's main straight is at least 1,120 px (`PIT_STRAIGHT_MIN`, tested), so a lane never bends round a corner: Crescent Park, Silver Heath and Ardennes were lengthened along their straights for it. Leave the track on the pit side at the entry and you're committed: the car drives itself down the entry road, slowing (not until it's off the track) to reach the first line at the 120 px/s limit, down the lane on the limiter, into your team's box while the crew fits new tyres and repairs its damage (1.2 s, plus up to 3 s for a wreck's worth of repairs; a team's second car queues behind its first), and back out to the second line and up the exit road to speed, handing it back where it meets the track's edge (an AI car keeps to that edge along the blend line, 400 px, before crossing to the racing line). Cars in the pits keep behind each other by how far down the lane each is (side by side on a bend, each knows which of them is ahead). The pit wall calls **BOX, BOX** on the way to the entry when a stop now pays (worn tyres, damage, or both, against the laps left), and the AI stops on the same sums. Passing a car in the pits under the safety car is no penalty. The rules are in `src/f1/pits.ts`.

**Replay:** a few seconds after you take the chequered flag, the race holds and your finish plays again: the 10 seconds up to just past the line, every car where it was (the safety car too), the camera on your car, slowing down as you cross the line, under a blinking ● REPLAY. A skips it. Then the in-lap carries on. Every car is recorded 20 times a second through the race, the last 15 seconds kept (`src/f1/replay.ts`).

**Grid pan:** before the start lights, the camera glides down the grid from pole to the back, a moment on each car, with its grid place, driver and team on the banner (yours in gold); then it swings back to your car and the lights come on. A skips it straight to the lights. Nothing moves and the lights wait while it plays.

**Final lap and the flag:** as you start your last lap the banner calls FINAL LAP, with a bell, and the strip under the screen says FINAL LAP until you finish. From the moment the winner crosses the line a chequered flag waves on the pit wall at the line (`src/f1/flag3d.ts`), for every car that finishes after; and as you take yours, a big waving chequered flag shows over the picture for a few seconds, with the fanfare.

**After the flag:** every car does an in-lap. Back at the pits, the top three carry on to their parking spots on the main straight, gold boxes numbered 1, 2 and 3 just past the line (P1 furthest on), and the rest drive down the pit lane to their team's box, where the crew push them back into the garage, nose out, side by side with their teammate. Your car drives itself round too, with the banner saying where it's going (IN LAP · PARK IN SPOT 1, or BACK TO THE GARAGE); once it's parked, the champagne ceremony follows. Or skip the in-lap: A (SKIP) finishes the race at once (anyone still racing gets the finish their pace would bring, everyone is put where their in-lap would end) and cuts straight to the ceremony; A skips the wait for the others after a DNF too.

**The champagne ceremony** (`src/f1/podium3d.ts`): a three-step podium on the run-off across the straight from the top three's spots (at Royal Park, up on the deck over the straight), the winner on the top step (gold), second on its left (silver), third on its right (bronze), each driver in their team's colours (your driver in the gold helmet). They bounce with their arms up, then spray champagne, golden jets arcing out of the bottles toward the middle, while confetti falls; the camera closes in, the banner names the three, and the podium march plays. The results follow after 7 s, or at A.

## Run it

```sh
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173
```

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `npm run dev` | Vite dev server with hot reload |
| `npm run build` | Typecheck + production build to `dist/` |
| `npm test` | Unit tests (Vitest) |
| `npm run typecheck` | TypeScript only |

**Controls:** each device drives the way it suits, and the game follows whichever you used last:
- **Touch:** the analogue thumbstick points where you want to go, and how far you push it is the throttle (full from about 85% of the way out). B drifts.
- **Keyboard:** the car itself: ↑ (or W) is the gas, ↓ (S) the brake (held once stopped, it reverses), ← → (A, D) steer, X or Shift drifts.
- **Gamepad:** the right trigger is the gas, the left the brake, the left stick steers (gently near the centre), A drifts, Start pauses, Y restarts, Back is SELECT and the d-pad works the menu.

The menu fits on one screen and is all touch (the deck is hidden there): the circuit is one card at a time (its outline, name, a line about it and your lap record, with dots under it for where it is in the list), swiped (or its ◀ ▶ edges tapped) to the next, and tapped in the middle to race; under it a compact row each for MODE, TEAM, WEATHER and QUALIFYING, swiped or tapped at the sides to change, then the big gold button that races (named for the mode) and SETTINGS. On a keyboard, up/down moves, left/right changes the circuit or a row, and Enter races. SETTINGS, under the rows, opens the settings: DIFFICULTY, STICK (the thumbstick on the LEFT or the RIGHT, with the A and B buttons on the other side, mirrored), VIBRATION and SOUND; DONE goes back. All are remembered. In a race, A pauses (so does switching to another app or tab), START restarts and SELECT (once let go) goes back to the circuits. The pause screen has RESUME, RESTART and CIRCUITS to tap, and A resumes. On a keyboard: X or Shift is B, Z or Space is A, Enter is START, Backspace is SELECT, Esc or P pauses, and V switches between the handheld and wide layouts.

**HUD:** the readout at the top left has your lap, last, best and record times; the gaps to the cars either side of you (`▲ RAZZ +0.84`, `▼ MOCHI −1.20`), timed at points round the lap as timing loops do (`src/f1/gaps.ts`); your car's health and tyres; and, small and dim, the speed, frame rate and picture quality. Gaining or losing a place lights up your position in the strip below the screen for a moment (green ▲, red ▼); a new fastest lap of the race is called in purple, and shown in purple in the results.

**Sound:** synthesised with Web Audio, so there are no sound files (`src/engine/audio.ts`, `src/f1/sounds.ts`): your engine, built as an engine sounds (a V6's firing pulses and the crank's other orders in one waveform, two banks a touch out of tune, a soft clip for grit and a breath of exhaust noise; climbing through eight gears to about 12,500 rpm, dropping back less at each upshift in the high gears, with the crack of the ignition cut, grittier on the throttle, popping and crackling on the overrun when you lift), the nearest rival's engine (louder as it closes, higher as it comes and lower as it goes), tyre squeal while sliding (a wavering tone over a hiss), a rumble on grass and gravel and a buzz over the kerbs, rain hiss in the wet, a thud for each hit (bigger for a harder one), a beep for each start light and a higher one at lights out, a fanfare at your flag, a chime for a lap record or your fastest lap, and menu clicks. Browsers only allow sound after a tap or a key, so it starts with the first one; pausing, or leaving the app or tab, silences it. SOUND in the settings sets the volume: OFF, LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH (remembered).

**Music:** an anthem for the landing screen (the menu): the game's theme song, "Triumph in Motion" (`public/music/theme.mp3`), a track for the race, a calmer one for after the flag and a march for the podium, looping, crossfading when they change: the race's gives way to the calmer one at your flag, and when the winner turns off for its spot on the straight (or you skip the in-lap) a triumphal march takes over as the top three drive into their places, on through the results. (Its placeholder, should the file not load, is an original tune in the style of a cinematic racing TV theme: D minor at 124 bpm, a driving 16th-note synth pulse under a heroic brass tune, string pads, a pumping octave bass and big drums.) The march is an original tune in the manner of a bullring parade, in the spirit of Bizet's "Les Toréadors": A major, dotted brass fanfares in thirds over an oom-pah tuba, a marching snare and cymbals. MUSIC in the settings sets its own volume (OFF, LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH; remembered); pausing, or leaving the app or tab, silences it with the sounds. The player is `src/engine/music.ts` and the tracks are listed in `src/f1/music.ts`. Until the composed tracks are in, each plays a placeholder: a chiptune loop written there as notes (the menu in A minor at 96 bpm, the race in E minor at 150 bpm, quieter under the engines) and synthesised on the device, so there's nothing to download. **To drop in a composed track:** put the file in `public/music/` (AAC `.m4a` or `.mp3` play everywhere, iOS included) and set the track's `file` (e.g. `file: 'music/race.m4a'`); with an intro before the loop or an ending after it, set `loopStart` and `loopEnd` in seconds, and the loop is gapless. `gain` sets the track's level in the mix. A file that's missing or won't play falls back to the placeholder.

**Vibration:** your car buzzes when it crashes (harder for a harder hit, long for a wreck) or lands hard, rumbles on grass and gravel, and patters over the kerbs (`src/f1/rumble.ts`); the deck's buttons tick under the finger. In the Android app it goes through Capacitor's Haptics plugin, in Android browsers through the Vibration API; iOS Safari has no vibration, so there it's silent. VIBRATION in the settings or on the pause screen turns it off (remembered).

**Records:** your best lap on each circuit, and your best race time there for each number of laps (penalties included), are kept on the device between races (`src/f1/records.ts`). Each lap is saved as soon as it's done. The menu shows each circuit's lap record, the race readout has it as REC, a lap that beats it is announced (NEW LAP RECORD), and the results show both records, marked NEW! when this race set them. Your best qualifying lap on each circuit is a record of its own, kept apart from the race's lap record (a flying lap on your own isn't a race lap, and a deleted one never counts): during qualifying REC shows it, and the qualifying table shows it, marked NEW! when you've just beaten it.

**Fixed-step simulation:** the race runs in steps of exactly 1/60 s, however fast or slow the screen draws (`src/engine/fixedStep.ts`): a frame runs as many steps as its time holds, and each car is drawn between its last two steps, so a 90 or 120 Hz screen still moves smoothly. A race comes out the same to the last digit on a 30 fps phone and a 144 Hz monitor, and the same as in the headless tests (which step at 1/60 s too); a frame too slow for 4 steps slows the game rather than stalling it. A race's random draws (the rival teams, the start-light wait) come from its seed (`src/engine/rng.ts`), so the same seed and the same driving play it again exactly: the ground for M3's ghosts and replays.

**Frame rate:** the readout at the top left shows the frames per second and the picture quality (HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW). When a phone can't keep up, the quality steps down; if even the lowest level is no quicker, the browser is holding the page to 30 fps (Low Power Mode, or Safari throttling a game embedded in another site's page, as on itch.io), so it goes back to where it started. Embedded like that, the menu offers **PLAY IN ITS OWN TAB**, which isn't throttled; saves carry over.

**TUNE:** the button at the top right of a race opens sliders for laps, AI opponents, an AI pace adjustment (× the difficulty's), camera zoom and look-ahead; changes apply at once and are kept on the device (Reset puts the defaults back).

**URL flags:** `?circuit=crescent-park` (or `silver-heath`, `harbour`, `royal-park`, `ardennes`) goes straight to a race (add `&mode=timetrial` for a Time Trial there), `?mode=championship` opens the Championship screen, `?inputlog` lists the input events the page receives and the buttons held (for debugging controls on a phone), `?seed=<n>` plays races from that seed (the same rivals and start; `__cc.seed()` gives the current race's), `?debug` shows the FPS / quality readout and exposes `window.__cc` for tests (`__cc.look(x, y)` holds the camera on a point of the map, `__cc.landmarks()` lists a street circuit's landmarks, `__cc.stands()` the grandstands, `__cc.wreck(3)` wrecks the car in P3, `__cc.toPits()` damages your car and puts it in the pit entry, `__cc.flag(1)` waves the chequered flag for everyone now with you in P1, to watch the in-lap), and `?desktop` / `?mobile` force a layout.

## Publishing to itch.io

`.github/workflows/itch.yml` tests and builds every push and pull request. A push to the default branch (or **Run workflow** in the Actions tab on it) also uploads `dist/` to itch.io with [butler](https://itch.io/docs/butler/) as the `html5` channel, versioned `<package version>+<commit>`.

Setup (once):
1. On itch.io, create the project with kind **HTML**. Under the embed options, tick **Mobile friendly** (portrait) and **Fullscreen button**, and set a viewport of about 390×844.
2. In the GitHub repo, under Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions, add the secret `BUTLER_API_KEY` (itch.io → Settings → API keys) and the variable `ITCH_TARGET` = `<itch-user>/<game-slug>`.
3. Push to the default branch. After the first upload, tick **This file will be played in the browser** on the upload in the project's edit page.

Until both settings exist, the workflow skips the upload and warns which one is missing.

## Android

The same web build, wrapped by [Capacitor](https://capacitorjs.com) into an Android app (`android/`, `capacitor.config.ts`): full screen, portrait, the screen kept on, played offline; the back button goes from a race to the circuit menu, then out.

`.github/workflows/android.yml` builds an APK (a download under the run's **Artifacts**) and uploads it to the itch.io page as the `android` channel, so it can be downloaded there on a phone. It runs on every push and pull request (and by hand: Actions → android → Run workflow); only a push to the default branch uploads to itch.io. To install, allow installs from your browser or files app when Android asks. Each build has a higher version code, so it installs as an update over the last.

**Signing:** without secrets, the APK is signed with `android/app/sideload.keystore`, a key committed to the repo so every build updates the last one (like Android's debug key, it isn't a secret, so don't rely on it for anything you publish widely). For your own key, create one once and keep it safe (an update must always be signed with the same key):

```sh
keytool -genkeypair -keystore release.keystore -alias cornercutters -keyalg RSA -keysize 2048 -validity 10000
base64 -w0 release.keystore      # the value for ANDROID_KEYSTORE_BASE64
```

then add the repo secrets `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_BASE64`, `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD`, `ANDROID_KEY_ALIAS` (`cornercutters`) and `ANDROID_KEY_PASSWORD`. Switching from the sideload key to yours changes the app's signature: uninstall the sideloaded build once before installing the first one signed with your key.

Locally (with the Android SDK and JDK 21): `npm run android:apk` builds `android/app/build/outputs/apk/release/app-release.apk`. `npm run android:art` redraws the launcher icons and splash screens (`tools/android-art.py`, needs Pillow).

## Layout

```
index.html        The handheld column: game screen (#screen) + control deck (#deck)
src/
  main.ts         Routes in the page: the circuit menu, or the race on ?circuit=<id>, without page loads
  engine/         Controls, deck, layout, storage, TUNE panel, view types, driving physics (driving.ts),
                  ground and collision (sim.ts), vehicle edits, render/ (HD-2D pipeline, quality, effects,
                  car models, daylight, sprites, textures)
  f1/             The race, race control (raceControl.ts: wrecks, safety car, penalties), pit lane and stops (pits.ts), circuit menu,
                  circuit layouts (layouts.ts), circuit builder and scene, race rules and tuning; uses only engine/
public/fonts/     Pixel font, Silkscreen (SIL Open Font License)
tests/            Driving, circuit, racing, race control, pit stops, controls, layout, storage, vehicle edits, code boundaries
```

Saves live in local storage under the `cc:` prefix, as one versioned document, `cc:save` (`src/engine/save.ts`; the game's format in `src/f1/save.ts`): **settings** (sound, vibration, thumbstick side, layout), **choices** (the menu's last mode, circuit, team, difficulty, weather and qualifying), **ghosts** (your Time Trial record laps), **championship** (the season in progress), **progress** (the circuits unlocked) and **records**. Each value is checked when it's read, so a missing or odd one falls back to its default. A change of format bumps the version and adds a migration from the one before, and an older save is brought up to date on its first read (the separate keys of before the save format are carried over once, then removed). A save from a newer version of the game (an app rolled back) is read but never written over, and one that can't be read is set aside as `cc:save.bad` rather than lost. Every write re-reads the stored save and changes just its one value, so two tabs don't undo each other. The TUNE values (`cc:tune:f1`) and vehicle edits (`cc:vehicles`) are dev tools and keep their own keys. The status strip under the screen shows your position and lap.
